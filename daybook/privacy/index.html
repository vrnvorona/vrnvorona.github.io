Privacy policy

Last updated: October 13, 2026

Daybook is a macOS app made by Vladislav Voronin. This policy explains what data Daybook uses, where it is kept and how you can remove it. In short: Daybook keeps your data on your Mac. There is no Daybook server, no account with us, no analytics, no tracking and no ads.

Data that stays on your Mac

  • Your tasks, projects, time slots, check-ins and settings are stored in a local database in ~/Library/Application Support/Daybook.
  • Backups, if you turn them on, are written to a folder on your Mac that you choose.
  • macOS Calendar (iCloud, Exchange and other calendars macOS shows), if you allow access, is read through Apple’s Calendar framework on your Mac. Daybook can add “Busy” events there for the time you lock.
  • Notifications are scheduled locally by macOS.

None of this is sent to the developer or to anyone else.

Google user data

If you connect a Google account, Daybook signs you in through Google in your browser and asks for these permissions:

  • https://www.googleapis.com/auth/calendar.calendarlist.readonly — Daybook reads the list of your calendars (name, color, whether you can write to it) to show them and let you turn each one on or off.
  • https://www.googleapis.com/auth/calendar.events — Daybook reads events in the calendars you have turned on (title, time, location, description, meeting link, organizer, attendees and your response) to show them next to your tasks, to keep the time they take free when planning, and for event alerts.

Daybook writes to your Google Calendar only as a direct result of something you do in Daybook:

  • answering an invitation (your response and an optional note);
  • changing the time or description of an event you organize;
  • locking a task, a time slot or lunch: Daybook creates a private “Busy” event in the calendar you chose under “Write locks to” (titled “Busy”, or with the task title if you turn that on), moves it when you move the block, and deletes it when you unlock.

How Google data is stored

Events are cached in the local database on your Mac so the calendar works offline. Your Google sign-in tokens are kept in the macOS Keychain. Data travels only between your Mac and Google’s servers, over HTTPS.

Sharing

Daybook does not share, sell or transfer Google user data to anyone. It is not used for advertising, not read by people, and not used to develop, improve or train artificial intelligence or machine learning models. The developer never receives it.

Daybook’s use and transfer of information received from Google APIs to any other app will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Removing access and data

  • Disconnect the account in Daybook (Settings › Accounts & calendars › ⋯ › Disconnect): Daybook deletes the “Busy” events it created in that account, revokes its access with Google, and removes that account’s tokens and cached events from your Mac.
  • You can also revoke access at any time at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • To remove everything Daybook stored, quit it, delete the app and the folder ~/Library/Application Support/Daybook.

Children

Daybook is not directed at children under 13.
